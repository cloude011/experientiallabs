Privacy policy

Experiential Labs. Last updated 2026-09-04.

Resolute Labs AI, Inc., a Delaware corporation operating as Experiential Labs ("Experiential Labs", "we", "us", or "our") publishes this privacy policy (the "Policy") to describe the personal data we collect through the website at experientiallabs.ai (the "Site"), how we use and disclose it, and the choices and rights you have in relation to it. "Personal data" means information that identifies you or could reasonably be linked to you, such as an email address, an IP address, or a cookie identifier. By visiting the Site you acknowledge that we collect, use, and disclose personal data as described in this Policy.

1. Scope

This Policy applies to the Site only. The Experiential Labs platform at platform.experientiallabs.ai (the "Platform") is a separate service that this Policy does not cover. Anything you do after signing in to the Platform, including creating an account, generating an API key, and sending requests through the gateway, is outside this Policy. Where the Site hands you off to the Platform, this Policy explains what the Site sends in the handoff, and this Policy stops there.

2. Personal data we collect

2.1 Information you give us

The Site has four forms. Each takes only what is listed here, and we receive what you type.

  1. Get API key. The buttons on the home page and elsewhere ask for an email address. Your browser is redirected to the Platform's signup page with the address included in the URL, together with the campaign parameters, click identifiers and referrer of your visit so the Platform can record which campaign led to the account, and the Platform creates the account. If you have accepted the optional category, the Site also records that the address belongs to our visitor id (section 6.1a); otherwise the Site does not store the address. Our analytics remove the address from the URL before recording the page view.
  2. Waitlist and evaluation requests. The custom models and intelligence pages ask for an email address so that we can follow up. The address is emailed to us through Resend and, if you have accepted the optional category, recorded against our visitor id (section 6.1a).
  3. Startup program application. The startups page asks for a company name, a team size band, an expected monthly AI spend band, and an email address. The application is posted to a private Slack channel through a webhook; if Slack cannot be reached, it is emailed to us through Resend instead. Your browser is then redirected to the Platform signup with the address and the campaign parameters of your visit included in the URL, and the section 6.1a record is made if you have accepted the optional category.
  4. YC sign-in. The unlisted page for Y Combinator founders asks for an email address, or lets you continue with Google or GitHub. The Site posts a notification to a private Slack channel containing the email address, or the name of the sign-in method you chose, and then redirects you to the Platform's sign-in, with the address and the campaign parameters of your visit in the URL when you chose email. No password or OAuth credential passes through the Site.

Booking a call. The "Book a call" links open a scheduling page hosted by Cal.com in a new tab. Whatever you enter there is collected by Cal.com under its own privacy policy, and Cal.com passes the booking details to us. Where the Site books a time on your behalf from a form, it sends your email address and the chosen time to Cal.com from our server.

Email. If you write to us, we keep the correspondence and whatever personal data it contains.

2.2 Information collected automatically

  1. Server logs. Our hosting provider, Vercel, records each request to the Site: IP address, browser user agent, the page requested, the referring page, and the time.
  2. Analytics. PostHog records page views, clicks on the Site's buttons and forms, device and browser details, approximate location derived from your IP address, and the URLs you visit. It sets the identifiers listed in section 5.
  3. Advertising measurement. Google Tag Manager loads the Reddit pixel, which records page views and conversion events against the _rdt_uuid identifier so that Reddit can attribute visits and signups to Reddit advertising.
  4. Visitor identification. The RB2B script sends information about your visit to RB2B. Section 2.3 describes what comes back.
  5. Preferences. The Site stores your theme choice, the country your request came from, and your consent choice in your browser, as listed in section 5.

2.3 Information from third parties

For visitors in the United States, RB2B matches a visit against its own records and returns business contact information about the visitor to us: name, company, and work email address, where RB2B holds them. RB2B sends the match to us, and we keep it as described in section 10. We do not receive a match for visitors outside the United States. You can stop this identification as described in section 6.

3. How we use personal data

We use the personal data described in section 2 to:

  1. respond to the request you made through a form, including reviewing a startup application, following up on an evaluation request, and handing you off to the Platform;
  2. operate, maintain, and secure the Site, including detecting and investigating abuse, and debugging errors;
  3. understand how the Site is used, which pages are read, and which calls to action lead to a Platform signup;
  4. measure whether our advertising on Reddit leads to visits and signups;
  5. identify businesses whose staff visit the Site so that we can contact them about the Platform;
  6. send you marketing about the Site or the Platform where you have asked for it or where the law allows it, with an opt-out in every message;
  7. comply with law, respond to lawful requests, and establish or defend legal claims; and
  8. enforce our terms of use and protect our rights.

We do not use personal data collected through the Site for automated decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects about you.

5.1 Tracking across other websites and over time

Third parties collect personal information about your online activities over time and across different websites when you use the Site. Reddit does so through the pixel that Google Tag Manager delivers: the _rdt_uuid identifier lets Reddit connect your visit here with your activity on Reddit and on other websites that carry its pixel. RB2B receives data about your visit and matches it against records it has assembled from other sources. Neither runs for a visitor who meets the consent banner until that visitor accepts, and either can be switched off on the Your Privacy Choices page or by the Global Privacy Control signal, as described in section 6.

5.2 Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

The Site does not respond to "Do Not Track" browser signals, for which there is no settled standard. It does honor the Global Privacy Control signal, as described in section 6.3.

6. Your rights and choices

6.1 The consent banner

Visitors in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland see a banner on their first visit. PostHog analytics, the Reddit pixel, and RB2B visitor identification stay off until you choose "Accept all". Choosing "Reject all" keeps them off. You can change the choice at any time on the "Your Privacy Choices" page.

6.1a Our own first-party visitor tracking

Separately from the vendors above, the Site records its own first-party event stream: which pages you visit and which buttons or forms you use, tied together by a visitor id we set (el_cdp_id) and, on your first visit, the campaign parameters or referrer that brought you here (el_cdp_attr). This is not shared with PostHog, Reddit, Google, or RB2B; it is stored in our own database (Neon, section 4) and used to understand which pages and channels lead to a signup. It is switched on and off by the same choice as the rest of this section, never before you make it, and cleared the same way when you refuse or withdraw. We retain these rows for 90 days. The visitor id cookie is set for experientiallabs.ai and its subdomains, so the Platform can connect a signup to the pages on the Site that led to it. If you submit a form that carries your email address while this category is on, we also record, in the same database, that this visitor id belongs to that email, so we can connect a later conversation to the visit that started it. That one record is kept under the form-submission retention in section 10; the email itself is handled as a form submission (sections 2.1 and 3) whether or not this category is on.

6.2 The "Your Privacy Choices" page

Any visitor can open the Your Privacy Choices page from the link of that name in the footer of every page, marked with the opt-out icon, and switch the optional category off. Doing so stops PostHog capture, withdraws advertising consent in Google Tag Manager so the Reddit pixel no longer fires, and stops the RB2B script from loading on later page views. The choice is recorded in your browser for 12 months and applies to that browser only. This page is also where a California resident opts out of the sale or sharing of personal information, as described in section 7.

6.3 Global Privacy Control

If your browser sends the Global Privacy Control ("GPC") signal, the Site treats it as an opt-out of the optional category (analytics, advertising measurement, and visitor identification) for that browser, and as a valid request to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information under the state laws described in section 7.

6.4 Browser settings

You can block or delete cookies and local storage for the Site through your browser settings. The Site works without them, though it will not remember your theme choice or your consent record.

6.5 Marketing email

The Site does not send marketing email. If we begin to, each message will carry an unsubscribe link, and you can also write to the address in section 14. Messages that answer a request you made, such as a reply to a startup application, are service messages and are sent regardless of marketing preferences.

6.6 Access, correction, and deletion

You can ask us to tell you what personal data we hold about you, correct it, delete it, or give you a copy, by emailing the address in section 14. We will confirm your identity by replying to the email address on record before acting on the request, and we will respond within the time the applicable law allows. An authorized agent may make a request on your behalf with your written permission, which we may ask to see.

8. EEA and UK legal bases

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, the EU General Data Protection Regulation and the UK General Data Protection Regulation (together, "GDPR") apply, and Experiential Labs is the controller of the personal data described in this Policy. We rely on the following legal bases:

  1. Consent (Article 6(1)(a)) for PostHog analytics, the Reddit pixel, RB2B visitor identification, and our own first-party visitor tracking (section 6.1a, including the record linking your email to our visitor id when a form gives us both), which are one optional category. These stay off until you accept them in the banner, and you can withdraw consent at any time on the "Your Privacy Choices" page. Withdrawal does not affect processing that took place before it.
  2. Legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) for server logs, security, abuse investigation, and debugging, where our interest in running a working and secure Site is not overridden by your interests or rights. You may object to this processing as described below.
  3. Contract (Article 6(1)(b)) for the steps you ask us to take through a form: creating your Platform account, reviewing a startup application, answering an evaluation request, or booking a call.
  4. Legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)) where we must keep or disclose personal data to comply with law.

Subject to the exemptions in applicable law, you have the right to access your personal data and receive a copy, to have inaccurate data rectified, to have data erased, to restrict processing, to receive the data you provided in a structured and machine-readable format, to object to processing based on our legitimate interests, and to withdraw consent. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the supervisory authority in your country of residence or work, such as the Information Commissioner's Office in the United Kingdom. To exercise any of these rights, email the address in section 14.
